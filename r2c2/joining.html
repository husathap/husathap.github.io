Current Algoma University Students

Undergraduate Students

Current undergraduate students may be able to complete Bachelors' theses under the supervision of Prof. Hu for course credits. Usually, a thesis course will run from September until April of the next year. Therefore, an interested student should reach out to Prof. Hu during the summer semester to get started. Alternatively, a student may ask for a directed study for a project course for a single semester. If paid opportunities (e.g., research assistant) become available, they will be advertised internally. It is important to note that a student cannot earn course credits from paid positions.

Contact Instruction

To apply for a for-credit position, please email Prof. Hu. For paid positions, please wait for an internal communication and then follow its instruction.

Algoma University Students in the Course-based Program

A graduate student in the course-based program can earn computing project courses by working with Prof. Hu. If paid opportunities exist, they will be advertised. It is important to note that the a student cannot earn money from the computing project courses, and they cannot earn course credits from a paid position. Alternatively, a current graduate student in the applied program can change their program to the thesis-based one (MSc). If the transfer is successful, the following will happen:

  • Duration: The program duration will be lengthened to 24 months.
  • Reduced Course Load: The student will need to take a reduced number of courses.
  • Stipend: The student will receive $22,000 per year. However, much of the stipend will be used to subsidize the tuition fee.
  • Thesis Defence Requirement: The student will need to write and defend a thesis.
General information about the MSc program is available here. It is important to note that a successful transfer is not always guaranteed. Please do not contact Prof. Hu for a transfer opportunity; if one becomes available, it will be advertised internally and not on this page.

Contact Instruction

To apply for a for-credit position, please email Prof. Hu. For paid positions or for a program transfer, please wait for an internal communication and then follow its instruction.

Volunteer

A current Algoma University student can also volunteer for professors. Please note though that Prof. Hu will usually not take volunteers because he believes that all students should be compensated. Nevertheless, there can be a few exceptions. For instance, Prof. Hu can help to advise students on how to get their papers published. Please email him for any question.

Contact Instruction

Please email Prof. Hu. Then, once there is an agreement, please contact Research Office.

Prospective Students of the Thesis-Based Master's Degree Program in Computer Science (MSc)

This section is only for a general guideline, and is subject to change. Prof. Hu is not hiring a graduate research-based student at the moment.

It is important to note that Algoma University has two types of Master's degrees in computer science:

    MCS: This is a course-based program. The entry requirement is lower. However, it is completely self-funded and may not meet the entry requirements of some PhD programs. While an MCS student may convert their program to MSc later on, this is not guaranteed. If the applicant is interested in this program, there is no need to read on. Instead, they should visit the sites for the 12- and 16-month programs instead. This is because the program does not require a supervisor arrangement.
    MSc: This a thesis-based program. It is subsidized, and is designed for students who want to obtain PhD degrees in the future. However, the application process is not straightforward. The prospective applicant should to read this section in full.

To apply for the thesis-based Master's degree program, the applicant must first ensure the following that they meet the entrance requirement. This means, they must have the correct Bachelor's degree, sufficiently high GPAs, and references. For an international applicant, they may also be asked to provide the result of an English test. The full requirement can be read below:

Program Requirements

If the applicant can meet the requirement, they should then understand where Algoma University campuses are located. Algoma University, as it turns out, has multiple campuses that are quite far apart from each other. There are two campuses with professors who can supervise students:

  • Sault Ste. Marie: The Sault Ste. Marie campus is the main campus and it is the campus where Prof. Hu is based in. Therefore, a MSc student who has successfully applied to work with Prof. Hu will likely need to move and settle in Sault Ste. Marie, Canada. It is important to note that despite its French name, Sault Ste. Marie is not located in Europe nor in Quebec. The applicant should also not confuse the city with Sault Ste. Marie, USA. While it is visible from the broadwalk from Canadian side, the city falls under the jurisdiction of Michigan and USA.
  • Brampton: Brampton, Canada is located close to Toronto. It is about 683 km away from the Sault Ste. Marie campus by car.

The applicant should then be aware of the funding package at Algoma University. Currently, the funding package is set to be at least $22,000 per year. This amount comes from two sources:

  • Supervisor's Grant (Graduate Research Assistantship): The supervisor must provide at least $10,000 per year. The student cannot cover this amount by their own private fund. This restriction may be waived if the student has their own scholarship (e.g., NSERC scholarships, foreign scholarships).
  • Graduate Teaching Assistantship: To get $12,000, the student must work as a graduate teaching assistant.

It is important to note that the university does not cover the tuition fee. Therefore, assuming the grant is $22,000 per year, a domestic student will actually only receive about $11,600 per year, and an international student is expected to incur an anual debt of around $3,600. An applicant should read this page on funding and tuition fees very carefully:

Tuition, Awards and Funding

After understanding all of the university's requirements and the funding package, the student should then choose the supervisor. This means reading Prof. Hu's webpage and some of his recent works carefully. One ready, the student may reach out to him by email. It is important to send him a human-written email. All AI-generated emails may be ignored or reported as spams. Once Prof. Hu has provided an approval, the applicant may list him as a supervisor on the application form. Please note that in the future, Prof. Hu will likely ask a prospective MSc student to fill out online forms.

Just because Prof. Hu has approved the applicant, it does not mean acceptance is guaranteed. Ultimately, the university will have the final say in the process.

Prospective Doctor of Philosophy Students

Algoma University does not offer a PhD program. To work with Prof. Hu, the student must first be accepted by Dalhousie University and become a member of GEMLab. Only then, the student may ask Prof. Hu to be a co-supervisor or a member of the student's examination committee. It is important to note that PhD funding at Dalhousie University is extremely limited. Therefore, all prospective applicants should apply for an external scholarship as well.

Student Exchange and Postdoctoral Fellowships

It is important to note that on some occassions, Prof. Hu may be able to host an exchange student from a different university. Any current university student or new graduate is welcome to contact Prof. Hu for advice. Often, international students can temporarily study in Canada through one of the Mitacs exchange programs. Some programs use a centralized application portal while some requires the applicant to reach out. If the latter is the case, please feel free to send Prof. Hu an email.

On some occassions, postdoctoral fellowships may become available. These positions will be advertised outside of this website. Please follow the instruction on the advertisement materials. Please note that anyone who wihses to become postdoctoral fellows should also apply for their own funding packages as well.
